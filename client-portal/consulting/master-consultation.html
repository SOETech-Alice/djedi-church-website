3. Finding #1: 508(c)(1)(A) — What It Actually Is

Critical Clarification

There Is No IRC § 501(c)(38)

The Internal Revenue Code § 501(c) contains paragraphs (1) through (29). Number 38 does not exist.

The likely source of confusion is IRC § 508(c)(1)(A), which is a completely different provision.

What § 508(c)(1)(A) Actually Says

"Subsections (a) and (b) shall not apply to churches, their integrated auxiliaries, and conventions or associations of churches."

In Plain English: Churches do NOT have to file Form 1023 to apply for tax-exempt status. They are automatically treated as 501(c)(3) organizations from the moment they are organized as churches.

Key Insight

§ 508(c)(1)(A) Is NOT a Separate Tax Status

It is an exception to application requirements. Churches are still 501(c)(3) organizations — they just don't have to apply.

As the IRS states in Publication 1828:

"Churches that meet the requirements of IRC Section 501(c)(3) are automatically considered tax exempt and are not required to apply for and obtain recognition of tax-exempt status from the IRS."

What § 508(c)(1)(A) Does NOT Do

  • ❌ Make churches "sovereign" entities
  • ❌ Exempt churches from federal tax law
  • ❌ Exempt churches from filing required returns
  • ❌ Create a special tax status outside of 501(c)(3)
  • ❌ Give churches immunity from all federal obligations

Why Many Churches Apply Anyway

  • Donor confidence — Contributors want assurance that donations are tax-deductible
  • Bank requirements — Banks often require an IRS determination letter
  • Grant eligibility — Many foundations require proof of 501(c)(3) status
  • Legal clarity — A determination letter provides clear evidence
  • State requirements — Some states require IRS recognition for state tax exemptions

4. Finding #2: 501(c)(8) Isn't Viable

IRC § 501(c)(8) provides tax exemption for fraternal beneficiary societies. Three mandatory requirements must ALL be met:

Requirement Djedi Church Status Qualifies?
Lodge System — Local branches chartered by parent org Unified organization, not federation of chapters ❌ No
Member Benefits — Life, sick, accident, or other benefits Spiritual/consciousness community, not insurance ❌ No
Fraternal Characteristics — Ritual, ceremony Practices are religious/spiritual, not fraternal ⚠️ Questionable

Verdict

501(c)(8) Is NOT Viable for Djedi Church

Structurally incompatible · Severely limits tax-deductible contributions · Eliminates § 7611 protections

Critical Issue: Tax-Deductible Contributions

Under IRC § 170(c)(4), contributions to 501(c)(8) organizations are tax-deductible ONLY if:

  • Made by an individual (not corporations)
  • Used exclusively for religious, charitable, scientific, literary, or educational purposes
  • The organization is a domestic fraternal society operating under the lodge system

This is a MAJOR disadvantage compared to 501(c)(3), where ALL contributions are generally tax-deductible.

5. Finding #3: Corporate Sovereignty Options

The sovereignty question: How can Djedi Church be structured to maximize protection and minimize government interference?

The answer: Sovereignty in the legal sense means operating within the law while minimizing unnecessary government oversight — not operating outside the law.

Option A: Corporate Sole

What: A legal entity consisting of a single incorporated office occupied by a single natural person.

Advantages: Single-person control, continuity, property protection, maximum religious autonomy

Disadvantages: No limited liability (individual IS the corporation), not recognized in all states

Best For: Property holding — combine with other structures for liability protection

Option B: Nonprofit Religious Corporation

What: A state-registered entity organized exclusively for religious purposes.

Advantages: Limited liability, perpetual existence, corporate veil, professional credibility, automatic 501(c)(3) if church

Disadvantages: Must file articles, maintain formalities, state fees

Best For: Primary entity — best combination of liability protection and tax benefits

Option C: Irrevocable Charitable Trust

What: A legal arrangement where assets are permanently transferred to a trustee for charitable/religious purposes.

Types: Charitable Trust, Irrevocable Trust, Land Trust (anonymous property), Dynasty Trust (multi-generational)

Best For: Holding real property, endowment, long-term asset preservation

Option D: LLC with Religious Purpose

What: A Limited Liability Company for asset holding and non-church business activities.

Types: Operating LLC (operations), Holding LLC (assets), Management LLC (governance)

Best For: Additional liability layer for business activities — do NOT use as primary church entity

11. What Djedi Church CAN and CANNOT Do

What Djedi Church CAN Do ✅

  • Operate as a church with automatic 501(c)(3) status under § 508(c)(1)(A)
  • Enjoy special audit protections under IRC § 7611
  • Receive tax-deductible contributions (up to 60% AGI for cash, 30% for property)
  • Organize according to religious doctrine and governance
  • Make religious decisions without government interference (within limits)
  • Claim ministerial exception for employment decisions
  • Hold property through corporate and trust structures
  • Operate business activities through LLC
  • Apply for state tax exemptions (property, sales, income)
  • Establish bank accounts and enter contracts
  • Engage in unlimited religious activities and programs
  • Maintain membership-based community structure
  • Receive grants and foundation funding
  • Operate with corporate liability protection

What Djedi Church CANNOT Do ❌

  • Claim to be "sovereign" from federal tax law
  • Refuse to file required tax returns
  • Engage in political campaign activity (support/oppose candidates)
  • Excessively lobby (no "substantial part" of activities)
  • Allow private inurement of net earnings
  • Operate for private benefit rather than public religious purpose
  • Claim immunity from all federal law
  • Refuse to comply with employment tax obligations
  • Ignore state filing requirements
  • Mix personal and organizational finances
  • Destroy records if contacted by IRS
  • Lie or mislead government officials
