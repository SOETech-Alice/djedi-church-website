1. Missouri Chapter 352 — Full Overview

Missouri Revised Statutes Chapter 352 is titled "Religious, Charitable and Educational Organizations." It is a chapter within Title XXI (Counties and County Seats) that provides a specific legal framework for organizing religious organizations, charitable organizations, and educational institutions.

Where Chapter 352 Sits in Missouri Law

Important: Chapter 352 and Chapter 355 are NOT mutually exclusive. A religious organization may organize under either chapter, depending on its needs.

2. § 352 Deep Dive — Key Statutory Provisions

§ 352.010 — Definitions

This section defines key terms used throughout Chapter 352. Important definitions include:

  • "Religious organization" — An organization organized for religious purposes, including churches, synagogues, mosques, and other houses of worship
  • "Charitable organization" — An organization organized for charitable purposes
  • "Educational organization" — An organization organized for educational purposes
  • "Trustee" — A person who serves on the governing board of the organization

§ 352.020 — Incorporation of Religious Organizations

Who May Incorporate:

Any number of persons (typically three or more) who are associated together for religious, charitable, or educational purposes may form a corporation under this chapter.

  • Requires a minimum number of incorporators (typically 3+)
  • Incorporators must share the religious, charitable, or educational purpose
  • The organization must have a defined purpose
  • The organization must have a name that includes "Church," "Religious Organization," or similar designation (for religious organizations)

§ 352.030 — Articles of Incorporation

The articles of incorporation must include:

  1. Name of the organization — Must be distinguishable from other registered entities in Missouri
  2. Purpose — Must state the religious, charitable, or educational purpose
  3. Registered agent — Must designate a registered agent in Missouri
  4. Principal office — Must state the location of the principal office
  5. Duration — Can be perpetual or for a stated term
  6. Trustees/directors — Names of initial trustees (if applicable)

§ 352.040 — Corporate Powers

Once incorporated, the organization has the following powers:

  1. Sue and be sued — Can bring and defend legal actions
  2. Hold property — Can own real and personal property
  3. Enter contracts — Can enter into binding agreements
  4. Receive gifts and donations — Can accept contributions
  5. Borrow money — Can incur debt
  6. Invest funds — Can invest organizational funds
  7. Adopt bylaws — Can create internal governance rules
  8. Elect officers — Can appoint officers to manage daily operations

§ 352.060 — Trustees/Directors

Composition:

  • The governing board is typically called "Trustees" under Chapter 352
  • Must have a minimum number (typically 3)
  • No maximum (but must be practical)
  • Must be natural persons (not other organizations)

Duties:

  • Fiduciary duty to the organization
  • Duty of loyalty
  • Duty of care
  • Duty to act in the organization's best interest

4. Can a Church Incorporate Under Chapter 352?

Short Answer: Yes. Chapter 352 was specifically designed for religious organizations. A church can absolutely incorporate under Chapter 352.

Requirements for a Church Under Chapter 352

  1. Minimum Incorporators: Typically 3 or more persons associated for religious purposes
  2. Religious Purpose: The articles of incorporation must state a religious purpose
  3. Name: Must include a designation indicating religious purpose (e.g., "Church," "Religious Organization," "Ministry")
  4. Registered Agent: Must designate a registered agent in Missouri
  5. Principal Office: Must state the location of the principal office in Missouri
  6. Bylaws: Must adopt bylaws governing internal operations
  7. Trustees: Must have a governing board (typically 3+ trustees)

5. Filing Requirements Under Chapter 352

Step-by-Step Filing Process

→Step 1: Prepare Articles of Incorporation — include name, purpose, registered agent, principal office, initial trustees, and incorporators
→Step 2: File with Missouri Secretary of State — P.O. Box 778, Jefferson City, MO 65102-0778 (filing fee ~$25–50)
→Step 3: Obtain EIN from IRS — churches are automatically exempt from Form 1023 under IRC § 508(c)(1)(A), but still need an EIN
→Step 4: Adopt Bylaws — governing internal operations, officers, meetings, fiscal year, conflict of interest
→Step 5: Hold Organizational Meeting — adopt bylaws, elect officers, authorize bank accounts
→Step 6: Open Bank Accounts — articles of incorporation, EIN letter, board resolution required
→Step 7: File for State Tax Exemptions — sales tax (Form 1746), property tax, income tax

6. Tax Exemptions Under Chapter 352

Federal Tax Exemptions

Under IRC § 508(c)(1)(A), churches are automatically exempt from the requirement to apply for 501(c)(3) status:

  • No Form 1023 required — Churches don't have to apply
  • Automatic 501(c)(3) status — From the moment the church is organized
  • Tax-deductible contributions — Donors can deduct contributions
  • Special audit protections — Under IRC § 7611

Missouri State Tax Exemptions

Income TaxMo. Rev. Stat. § 143.042File Missouri Form 1746; automatic for qualifying organizations

Key Point: The choice between Chapter 352 and Chapter 355 does NOT affect federal or state tax exemptions. Both provide the same tax benefits.

Why Apply for IRS Recognition Anyway?

  • Donor confidence — Contributors want assurance that donations are deductible
  • Bank requirements — Banks often require an IRS determination letter
  • Grant eligibility — Many foundations require proof of 501(c)(3) status
  • Legal clarity — A determination letter provides clear evidence

Risk Mitigation Strategies

  1. Use an Attorney: Have an attorney review all documents before filing
  2. Maintain Records: Keep meticulous records of all meetings, resolutions, and transactions
  3. Follow Bylaws: Always follow the bylaws strictly
  4. Hold Regular Meetings: Hold regular board meetings and keep minutes
  5. Separate Finances: Keep church finances separate from personal finances
  6. Get Insurance: Consider directors and officers (D&O) insurance

11. Recommendation

Option A: Chapter 352 (Recommended for Simplicity)

Best if: Djedi Church wants a simpler, more traditional structure; Christopher wants minimal government oversight; the church will remain small and focused.

Purpose-built for religious organizations
Simpler requirements, lower cost, traditional structure

Option B: Chapter 355 (Recommended for Growth)

Best if: Djedi Church plans to grow significantly; Christopher wants maximum corporate protection; the church may seek grants or large donations.

Modern corporate protections
More familiar to banks and attorneys, more legal precedents, more flexible

Final Recommendation

For Djedi Church, Chapter 352 is recommended for simplicity, mission alignment, and cost-effectiveness. However, if Djedi Church plans to grow significantly or seek large grants, Chapter 355 may be the better long-term choice. The most important thing is to incorporate — regardless of which chapter you choose.
